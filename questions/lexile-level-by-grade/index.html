How should you use Lexile levels by grade?

A Lexile measure describes a reader’s assessed reading ability or a text’s measured complexity on the same scale. A grade comparison places a student’s measure alongside a reference group at a particular time of year. It is a comparison tool, not a grade assignment or a complete judgment of reading proficiency.

What does the official grade chart show?

MetaMetrics publishes grade charts that distinguish grade, testing season and percentile. Use the current official chart rather than an undated conversion copied into a worksheet. A percentile comparison answers a different question from whether a child understands the books assigned in class. There is no single Lexile number that every student in a grade must reach.

  1. Find the assessment date and reported measure on the school report.
  2. Match the season and grade in the official chart linked below.
  3. Ask the teacher what the result means alongside classroom work.

How do you choose books without treating the score as a label?

Start with the reader’s interests, purpose and comfort with the subject. Preview a passage together and ask the reader to explain it. A technically accessible book can still contain unfamiliar ideas or age-inappropriate material. A more demanding book can be a shared reading choice with support. Record the title and what help was useful instead of turning each reading session into another test.

What else should you know?

Does a Lexile measure diagnose a reading difficulty?

No. Discuss concerns with the teacher or an appropriately qualified professional; a measure alone does not explain why a reader is struggling.

Can I convert a Fountas & Pinnell letter directly into a Lexile number?

Do not treat a conversion as an individual assessment. Ask which assessment produced the score and use guidance for that specific system.
