How can you practise reading comprehension online?

Reading comprehension practice is the process of reading a passage, explaining its meaning and checking an answer against the text. Online practice can provide passages and questions, but useful work happens when the reader identifies evidence, notices confusion and revises an explanation. A completion score alone does not show how an answer was reached.

What should a useful practice activity include?

Choose an activity with a readable passage, questions that refer to it and feedback that explains the reasoning. Keep the passage visible while answering. Reading Rockets describes monitoring understanding and asking questions as strategies educators can teach explicitly. The routine below is an example you can adapt; it is not a validated test or a promise of improvement.

  1. Preview the title and state what you expect to learn.
  2. Read a manageable section and explain it in your own words.
  3. Answer a question and point to the sentence supporting it.
  4. Reread a confusing part and revise the explanation.

How can you check an answer rather than guess?

Separate an answer stated directly in the text from an inference. For an inference, name the clue and explain the connection. If feedback only marks an answer wrong, return to the passage before trying another option. Write down the unfamiliar word or sentence that caused trouble. That gives a teacher, tutor or practice partner something concrete to discuss.

How do you choose an activity for the reader?

Preview the content for topic, language and accessibility. Use a subject the learner wants to understand and adjust the passage length if maintaining attention becomes difficult. A reader who understands a spoken explanation but struggles with the printed words may need different support from someone who reads the words easily but misses the argument. Keep examples of both the passage and the response when asking for help.

What else should you know?

Is a multiple-choice score a reading level?

It is a result for that activity. A grade-level interpretation needs an assessment designed and interpreted for that purpose.

Can I practise without an app?

Yes. Use a short article or book section, explain its main point and support your explanation with details from the text.
